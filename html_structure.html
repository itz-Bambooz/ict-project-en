City Overview

Geographic location and community essence

🌾

Geographic Heart of the Delta

Sirs El-Layyan is located in the southern heart of the Menofia Governorate in the fertile Nile Delta region of Egypt. Surrounded by rich green agricultural fields, it stands between the Rosetta and Damietta branches of the Nile.

Originally established as an agricultural settlement, it evolved into an administrative city while keeping its rural traditions, close-knit family values, and community solidarity intact.

🏛️

Culture & Economy

Historically dominated by farming practices—including wheat, corn, and cotton cultivation—the city transformed during the mid-20th century into an educational epicenter for rural development across the Middle East.

"Sirs El-Layyan bridged traditional Egyptian village life with modern adult education and community development."

History & Heritage

From ancient roots to modern Egyptian prominence

Historical Roots

The name Sirs El-Layyan originates from ancient Coptic and Arabic roots tied to water channels and lush green canals ("El-Layyan" referring to fertile, moist soil irrigated by the Nile).

Throughout the Ottoman period and the reign of the Muhammad Ali Dynasty, the city developed as a reliable producer of agricultural staples for Lower Egypt. Its residents established local markets that supplied neighboring towns across Menofia.

Chronological Milestones

  • Ancient & Medieval Era
    Nile Irrigation Settlement
    Thrived as an agricultural village utilizing seasonal flooding and ancient canal systems.
  • 19th Century
    Administrative Development
    Expanded under modern Egyptian administrative reforms into a formal municipal center.
  • 1952

  • 2000s - Present

    Continues its legacy as an independent administrative city within the Menofia Governorate.

The ASFEC Center (UNESCO)

Arab States Fundamental Education Centre

📚

Why Sirs El-Layyan Became Famous Worldwide

In 1952, the Egyptian government, in cooperation with UNESCO, established the Arab States Fundamental Education Centre (ASFEC) in Sirs El-Layyan. The goal was to combat illiteracy, train educators, and promote rural community development throughout the Arab region.

Key Objectives of ASFEC

  • Training specialists in adult literacy and rural extension work.
  • Creating customized educational materials for rural Arab communities.
  • Conducting field research on socio-economic development in rural areas.

Regional Impact

Thousands of educators from across Egypt, Saudi Arabia, Jordan, Iraq, Sudan, and other Arab nations graduated from Sirs El-Layyan, making the city a household name in Middle Eastern educational history.
